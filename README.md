# token-radar

A radar that ranks token-less protocols by **expected value** — the fitted value if a token launches, times the model's odds that one arrives — built from a fixed DefiLlama snapshot of 758 protocols. Two models do the work, both fitted on the snapshot itself and both validated out of sample.

The value model (radar-4) prices a launch by least squares in log space on the snapshot's own already-issued protocols: 92 comparables that pass the board's own "a protocol in its own right" reading. It found that value grows far slower than TVL (a size-curve slope of 0.376, not 1 — among issued protocols the median market value is 0.87x TVL for the smallest third, 0.25x for the middle third, 0.08x for the largest), that the 7-day change carries no usable pricing signal (pre-launch TVL spikes are usually rented, points-driven capital; the term is dropped by a sign rule), and that age and chain reach adjust the curve slightly. Its out-of-sample median pricing error is a x3.49 typical miss, against x3.62 for a bare power law, x4.48 for the hand-set radar-2 and x4.74 for a naive flat multiple. The issuance model is a logistic regression over the snapshot's 290 clearly-labelled protocols scoring P(token) — the share of like-shaped protocols that have already issued — with a cross-validated AUC of 0.71 and its out-of-fold calibration deciles printed on the page (predicted against actual in every decile; the tails wobble inside the noise of n=290, so no recalibration is fitted, and age x family interactions tested on the same folds were rejected — AUC 0.707 against 0.709, Brier unchanged).

**Board edition 5 changes the ranking, and only the ranking.** Editions 1–4 sorted by launch value and showed the expected value in a side table. Edition 5 promotes the product: board.json's `implied_value_usd` IS P(token) x launch value, and the launch value rides as its own field (`launch_value_usd`), one visible multiplication away in every detail view. A $30M launch at 15% odds is a smaller prize than an $18M launch at 80% odds, and a ranking that ignores the odds answers a question nobody with capital asks. Neither fit moved — the value spec and the issuance model are the edition-4 fits, bit for bit — and the selection did not move either: every standalone pre-token protocol in the snapshot is on the board (198 of 198), so the new definition can only reorder. The farm plans were already priced on the product; edition 5 admits in the board's own ordering what the plans always assumed. The bootstrap bands were regenerated to match: 200 refits now resample both models together and re-rank by the product, so a row's rank range describes the ranking the board actually uses.

The first screen was rebuilt to show the work instead of describing it: a KPI strip (rows ranked, watchlist 40/40, the held-out miss, the top expected value, the farm count, the best expected net per hour), a self-check seal the page recomputes in the reader's browser on every load (row count, ordering, watchlist coverage, board total), and the full-universe audit as one segmented bar — 758 = 198 ranked + 416 already trading (they fit the curves; never ranked) + 144 set aside, each of the 144 listed below with its reason. The evidence rail beside the board carries the ablation the earlier editions put in this README: the per-family offsets radar-3 shipped flattered themselves on the canonical folds (0.515) but lost under 20 repetitions of 5-fold CV (0.551 against 0.527 without), so they are gone, and the paired-bootstrap interval that puts the shipped model's edge over a bare power law at indistinguishable-from-zero is printed where the ranking is, not behind it — along with the caveat that the spec was chosen on the same resamples it is scored on.

Every row's farm plan is priced the way a farmer prices it: expected profit at realistic capital ($250–$5,000 by TVL tier) from a declared 10% community share, pro-rata to capital against TVL, minus gas at 2025 post-Dencun levels and a 4%-a-year opportunity cost — then divided by the hands-on hours the plan asks for, because an hour is the scarcest input. P(profit) integrates over the fit's own out-of-sample residuals; where the expected yield per dollar sits under the opportunity rate, the plan says plainly that no size breaks even (74 rows). Verdicts: farm 27, thin 7, skip 164. Plans are written to 2025 practice — an established wallet funded from an exchange, time-weighted positions held across weekly snapshots, varied amounts on varying weekdays, a points ledger, an eligibility-checked claim inside its window. Each detail view builds its headline figure step by step (TVL, size term, age term, chain term, launch value, x P(token), expected value) and sets it against the three nearest traded comparables with the caps they actually reached. Two ranges are never merged: the bootstrap band (fit uncertainty) and the prediction interval from the folds' residuals (÷9 to x15), because launches land in the second, not the first. The blind spot is stated on the board: revenue, float and unlock design, and funding are what practitioners weight most, and none of them is among the snapshot's ten fields.

## Run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=./output node main.ts --input ./snapshot.json
```

To reproduce the models' fitted constants, their out-of-sample validation (including the offsets ablation, the per-term ablation and the issuance calibration), the joint bootstrap bands, the issuance weights and the residual distribution (written to lib/bands.ts, lib/ptoken.ts and lib/residuals.ts):

```sh
node tools/fit.mjs ./snapshot.json
```
